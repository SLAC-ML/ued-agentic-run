Collimator alignment and gun ramp-up, simulated

Fuhao's kicker sketch, the SSUED schematic and the current gun ramp script are now a beamline model with all twelve steering kickers, a Python runner for scans, and a simulation for each bring-up stage. Both stages behave the way their procedures expect. What's missing is mostly calibration data.

Simulation only. Nothing here wrote to the machine. On ued-daq the work was read-only: reading config files and PV lists, and caget of hutch PVs. Nothing was run on testfac-srv01. The GPT runs and the ramp procedure ran on S3DF (iana), and the "gun" in the ramp tests is a Python model.

  • collimatorA 150 µm laser-spot error on the cathode makes the whole beam miss the 100 µm hole. XC/YC00 bring the transmission back to 6.4 %, and XC/YC01 put the beam within 26 µm of the ANDOR1 centre.
  • ramp · beamThe beam barely leaves the gun below AREQ ≈ 0.13. From there the energy rises almost linearly to 3.1 MeV at 0.53, assuming AREQ is linear in field.
  • ramp · procedureThe ported ramp completes a normal ramp, and aborts cleanly on a cooling-water fault and on a feedback trip. The original script would keep ramping through the feedback trip.

Stage: gun ramp-up

Two layers. The procedure, which only touches RF, runs against a virtual gun. The beam the gun produces along the way comes from GPT.

The procedure, on a virtual gun

bringup/gun_ramp.py does what ramp_rf.py does. It sets AREQ to 0.05, turns on both feedbacks, then steps AREQ to the target in 300 steps of 2 s. Before every step it waits for the gun body temperature to be within 33–37 °C, and aborts to AREQ = 0 after 10 readings out of range in a row. It adds two checks: the feedbacks must read Active before the ramp and at every step, and the amplitude readback must track the setpoint.

How the cooling-water fault is made

The virtual gun's body temperature relaxes toward T_water + k·AACT² (water temperature plus RF heating), with a 90 s time constant. The fault raises T_water from 34.6 to 37.6 °C at t = 300 s. The body warms toward about 38.4 °C, crosses 37 °C at 6.5 min, and the procedure's temperature gate holds the ramp. Ten readings later, 2 s apart, it gives up and sets AREQ to 0.

How the feedback trip is made

At t = 400 s the model marks the amplitude feedback as tripped. From then on AFBST reads Tripped instead of Active, and the drive is held: the amplitude readback stops following the rising setpoint. The new per-step status check catches it at the next step. ramp_rf.py never reads the status during the ramp, so it would carry on.

What the model is not

Every constant in sim/virtual_gun.py is a placeholder chosen to stay inside the window at 0.53: the water temperature, the heating coefficient, the 90 s time constant, the readback lag and noise. The model tests the procedure's logic, not the machine's real timing. The fault times are arbitrary.

The beam along the ramp

The same deck, with the gun field scaled by AREQ and the phase (37°) and solenoids at the operating point. Assumption: field is linear in AREQ, with 0.53 giving the deck's 68 MV/m (128.3 MV/m per unit). Fitting this curve to a spectrometer energy measurement at a few setpoints would give the real calibration.

XC/YC01 can only centre one plane. With the beam on ANDOR1, sample 1 is still about 160 µm off. Hitting both needs XC/YC02 as well. That's worth deciding before this becomes an agent tool.
